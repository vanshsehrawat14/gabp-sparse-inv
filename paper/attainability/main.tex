{\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}

\begin{quote}\small
Many machine-learning models require a global linear solve. For sparse
coupling with fixed block size and elimination width, selected inversion computes inverse blocks on the
factor's filled pattern in $\bigO(n)$ time, and its projected derivative has
a reverse schedule with the same asymptotic structural work. The central
question is whether an
exact structured solve acts as an \emph{inductive bias}, selecting a
better-generalizing solution when competing models attain comparable
training fit. Two preregistered matched-fit probes test this hypothesis on
$30$ held-out seeds: routed-field size extrapolation and deep-equilibrium
(DEQ) training near spectral instability. The exact arms win the raw
comparisons, but the finite Jacobi and Neumann alternatives match their
training fit in only $0$--$23.3\%$ of high-difficulty seeds, below a
preregistered $60\%$ bar, so the equal-fit comparison never arises. In the
routed field, a source-generated support certificate extends the result beyond
Jacobi: even after granting a generic competitor all seven charged products,
its label-only test-MSE floor is $578$ times the exact arm's error. The
sparse-direct backward remains within
$1.1\times10^{-12}$ of a dense 64-bit floating-point (fp64) oracle where
32-term Neumann adjoints have
$77.1\%$ and $97.4\%$ relative error at $\rho(J)=0.99$ and $0.999$.
A post-hoc adversarial study on 20 fresh development seeds and
$256$-dimensional systems shows that this failure is rule-sensitive. At eight
matched products, unrestarted GMRES clears the comparable-fit bar in $18/20$
and $15/20$ hard-cell seeds, versus $5/20$ and $4/20$ for Neumann; its median
exact-forward test-loss ratios to the exact arm are $1.001$ and $0.996$. Two
predeclared positive predictions fail, while the easy control passes. A failed
DEQ control, conflicting maze-floor diagnostics, and a later reused-seed
analysis keep the causal conclusion hedged. Exact structured computation
expands what the tested truncations can attain in these solve-shaped CPU
probes, but the proposed inductive-bias explanation is not supported.
\end{quote}
