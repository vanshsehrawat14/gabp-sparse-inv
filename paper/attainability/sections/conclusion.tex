\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Selected inversion is classical. On the measured finite-precision grid, this
implementation has no robust accuracy advantage over a well-ordered dense
solve; its role here is the exact structured computation tested in the two
probes.

The tested hypothesis was that exact structured computation acts as an
inductive bias among models with comparable training fit. In both
preregistered probes over $30$ held-out seeds, the finite alternative fails
to attain comparable fit in the high-difficulty cells: bounded Jacobi sweeps
in Section~\ref{sec:maze} and finite Neumann adjoints in
Section~\ref{sec:deq}. The separate three-seed cross-architecture diagnostic
is exploratory and does not alter this attribution. The DEQ low-difficulty
control fails its relative-gap clause; the maze extrapolation-floor comparison
passes but the in-distribution \texttt{floor\_sound} diagnostic fails, leaving
NC2 ambiguous. The later reused-seed analysis is excluded. The supported
conclusion is narrow and hedged: against the truncated rules tested, exact
structured computation expands the attainable regime on these controlled,
solve-shaped tasks, while the inductive-bias explanation is not supported.
Proposition~\ref{prop:reach} extends the routed-field half to the class it
covers; residual-optimal rules on the DEQ side are the successor study.

The evidence is CPU-scale and confined to low-treewidth problems. Exact selected
uncertainty and difficult-spectrum implicit gradients require new protocols;
none of the results extends beyond the measured regimes
(Section~\ref{sec:program}).
