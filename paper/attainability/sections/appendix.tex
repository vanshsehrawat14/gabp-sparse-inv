\section{The tree selected-inverse schedule and its projected adjoint}
\label{app:theorem}

This appendix states the classical machinery used in
Section~\ref{sec:theorem}: the tree recurrence that makes the layer exact and
global, and the projected-Hessian argument for its first-order adjoint.
The formal statements below are confined to that tree specialization and
projected first-order adjoint. The filled-pattern symmetric and static-pattern
non-symmetric implementations are tested reference software; this appendix
does not claim or prove a new general recurrence.

\subsection{Forward: a two-pass schedule on the elimination tree}

\begin{theorem}[Tree selected inverse]
\label{thm:tree-forward}
Let $\Gr$ be a tree rooted at $r$ and $A$ SPD with pattern $\Gr$. Order nodes
so that each precedes its parent. Then \textbf{(a) zero fill:} the block
$LDL\T$ factorization in this order has $L$ unit lower-triangular with the
pattern of $A$ and every pivot $\piv{v}$ SPD; \textbf{(b) collect
(leaves$\to$root):}
$\piv{v} = \Ablock{v}{v} - \sum_{c\in\children(v)} \Ablock{v}{c}\inv{\piv{c}}\Ablock{c}{v}$
and $\Gblock{r}{r} = \inv{\piv{r}}$; \textbf{(c) distribute
(root$\to$leaves):} with $\fac{v} = \Ablock{\parent(v)}{v}\inv{\piv{v}}$,
\[
  \Gblock{\parent(v)}{v} = -\,\Gblock{\parent(v)}{\parent(v)}\,\fac{v},
  \qquad
  \Gblock{v}{v} = \inv{\piv{v}} + \fac{v}\T \Gblock{\parent(v)}{\parent(v)} \fac{v}.
\]
This produces every block of $\selinv(A)$ exactly in
$\bigO((|V|+|E|)\,b^3)$ time and $\bigO((|V|+|E|)\,b^2)$ memory. Steps
(b)--(c) are the tree specialization of the classical Gaussian-BP
collect/distribute and Takahashi recurrences
\citep{weiss2001correctness,takahashi1973}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Why the derivative has a same-pattern reverse schedule}

\begin{theorem}[Projected selected-inverse derivative is self-adjoint]
\label{thm:p0}
On the SPD cone, under the full Frobenius inner product on symmetric
matrices, $\selinv_{\pat}(A) = P_{\pat}\nabla_A \log\det A$.
Consequently the Fr\'echet derivative
$D\,\selinv|_A = P_{\pat}\,L_A\,\iota_{\pat}$ (with
$L_A: X\mapsto -\inv{A}X\inv{A}$ the derivative of inversion and $P_{\pat}$
the on-pattern projection, and $\iota_{\pat}$ the inclusion of symmetric
on-pattern perturbations) is self-adjoint: it is the projected Hessian of a
scalar potential, and a gradient field has a symmetric Jacobian (symmetry of
second derivatives).
Hence, for the tree schedule above, the reverse-mode map
$\cotan{G}\mapsto\cotan{A}$ equals the forward derivative map and is realized
by reversing the schedule of Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-forward} at the same structural work
(cheap-gradient principle \citep{baur1983complexity}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Self-adjoint up to transpose; the non-symmetric correction]
\label{rem:transpose}
For non-symmetric $A$,
$L_A^* : X\mapsto -A^{-\mathsf{T}}XA^{-\mathsf{T}} = L_{A\T}$, so the adjoint
is $P_{\pat}L_{A\T}\iota_{\pat}$: the projected inversion derivative
evaluated at $A\T$. It is not itself the selected inverse of $A\T$. The
non-symmetric (LU / Erisman--Tinney) kernels are therefore not literally
self-adjoint; the derivative is adjoint up to transpose, which is why
their backward propagates independent lower and upper factor cotangents (the
$L$ of $A$ is the $U$ of $A\T$).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the naive off-pattern argument fails]
It is tempting to argue that $\cotan{A}|_{\pat}$ never references an
off-pattern block of $\inv{A}$. That is false:
$\cotan{A}_{vv} = -\sum_{(s,t)\in\pat} \Gblock{v}{s}\cotan{G}_{st}\Gblock{t}{v}$
involves off-pattern $\Gblock{v}{s}$. What is true is that the reverse
schedule never forms them: it propagates cotangents along tree edges, exactly
as the forward computes $\Gblock{v}{v}$ without forming the dense inverse.
The first-order reverse schedule closes on the pattern. This statement alone
does not establish pattern closure or the cost of arbitrary higher-order
derivatives.
\end{remark}

\section{Engine details: generality, stability boundary, and a drop-in}

\paragraph{No-pivot stability boundary (non-symmetric).}
The non-symmetric kernels eliminate on a static pattern with no pivoting,
valid while pivots stay non-singular.
On the tested sweep this is at parity with a pivoted dense LU while the
system is block-diagonally dominant, with the accuracy floor collapsing below the
dominance boundary. The SPD experiment shows a small reconstructed-factor
residual on the finite tree/conditioning grid tested; it does not prove a
height- or $\kappa$-independent global backward-error theorem. The
non-symmetric diagnostic additionally exposes the no-pivot boundary. These
numerical-analysis observations have no demonstrated learning payoff. Data:

\paragraph{DeltaNet chunk inverse (a drop-in capability note).}
The differentiable triangular chunk inverse $(I-A)^{-1}$ drops, unchanged,
into a chunked delta-rule linear-attention layer
\citep{yang2024parallelizing}: the within-chunk delta rule \emph{is} that
triangular solve. It reproduces the token-by-token delta rule exactly, and
its analytic backward equals autograd through the whole multi-chunk layer.
Because the forward is identical by construction, this demonstrates
availability (the operator is a genuine drop-in), not a capability gain. A
learned-capability payoff for
sequence models would be a learned-sparse graph-coupled mixer, which belongs
to the program of Section~\ref{sec:program} rather than to the present
evidence.

\section{Reach and saturation of a source-Krylov budget}
\label{app:reach}

This appendix proves Proposition~\ref{prop:reach} and states the complementary
bound that limits it from above. Together they delimit the range of budgets
over which a fixed-$K$ experiment carries any information about the budget.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reach}]
Let $V_p$ be the span of the vectors available after $p$ products. By the
access model, $V_0\subseteq\spn\{c\}$. If
$V_{p-1}\subseteq\spn\{c,Mc,\dots,M^{p-1}c\}$, the next product applies $M$ to
some vector in $V_{p-1}$ and therefore lies in
$\spn\{Mc,\dots,M^pc\}$; subsequent linear combinations keep $V_p$ inside
$\spn\{c,Mc,\dots,M^pc\}$. Induction gives the claimed Krylov span. One
multiplication by $M$ moves support along at most one edge of its undirected
pattern, so $\supp(M^ic)\subseteq\hop_i(\supp c)\subseteq\hop_i(\supp b)$.
The support and pointwise-error conclusions follow.
\end{proof}

The bound is sharp, and needs no genericity. On a path graph with $c=b=e_1$ and
every edge weight nonzero, reaching vertex $P+1$ from vertex $1$ in $P$ steps
requires traversing the unique shortest path, so the hop-$P$ entry of $M^{P}c$
is the product of those $P$ weights and cannot vanish. Cancellation on a graph
with cycles can only shrink a support, never extend one, so it does not
threaten the bound from the other side.

The access model is load-bearing. If a rule may apply a fixed global transform
to $b$, then $\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}b$ is globally supported before
any product. If it may probe the operator with arbitrary vectors, it can exploit
structural orthogonality \citep{curtis1974estimation}: columns whose patterns
do not overlap are recovered together from one product against their common
indicator. A tridiagonal operator is therefore recovered exactly in three
products and a five-point grid stencil in five, after which the rule solves
exactly with no further product and returns a globally supported answer at any
diameter. Such
rules reconstruct the operator instead of approximating its inverse from a
source, and belong on the exact side of the comparison this paper draws.
The routed-field arm carries no global transform inside its sweep path, and
\href{https://github.com/vanshsehrawat14/gabp-sparse-inv/blob/main/tests/test_reach_bound.py}
{\nolinkurl{tests/test_reach_bound.py}} checks the support property on the shipped arm.

Within the class it covers, the bound is indifferent to the method. Damped
Jacobi and truncated Neumann are fixed polynomials; zero-start
unpreconditioned GMRES and conjugate gradients choose their coefficients
adaptively but remain in the same source-generated span. For a preconditioned
method, every application of the preconditioner must be included in the access
model. Diagonal scaling adds no reach, whereas triangular solves can propagate
across the graph despite sparse factors. The bound applies only to the total
reach of the operators actually used.

\begin{remark}[Saturation, the complementary bound]
\label{rem:saturation}
For the unpreconditioned system, let $d$ be the grade of $b$ with respect to
$A$, the degree of its minimal annihilating polynomial. For nonsingular $A$
that polynomial has nonzero constant term, so $\inv{A}b$ is a combination of
$b,Ab,\dots,A^{d-1}b$. The span therefore saturates after the $(d-1)$st
power. That is not the same as a black-box solver terminating after $d-1$
products: a residual-optimal rule generally spends the next product to
determine the residual-minimizing coefficients. GMRES \citep{saad1986gmres}
is guaranteed to terminate by $d$ products in exact arithmetic, as is CG in
the SPD setting. The scalar case already
shows why the distinction matters: $A^{-1}b\in\spn\{b\}$, but no zero-query
rule can infer the coefficient $A^{-1}$. When $I-M$ is nonsingular, the same
argument applies to a stationary splitting with iteration matrix $M$ and
source $c$, replacing the target by $(I-M)^{-1}c$.
Since $d\leq n$, $n$ black-box products reach the generic exact-termination
ceiling. An invariant
subspace, a minimal polynomial of degree below $n$, and lucky breakdown each
lower $d$ rather than raise it, so the threshold is conservative in the only
direction that matters here. A measurement past saturation cannot separate
``the budget is too small'' from ``this particular polynomial is a poor one,''
and reach is similarly uninformative once the budget exceeds the graph
diameter. A fixed budget carries information about the budget only while it is
below both thresholds. On the sharpness construction above, a nonzero-weight
path of $n$ nodes with an endpoint source, the two coincide at $n-1$: the
diameter is $n-1$ and the grade is $n$. That equality belongs to that
construction, not to path graphs in general, since a smaller grade lowers the
Krylov threshold while leaving the diameter unchanged.
\end{remark}

Applied to this paper: the affine gradient sweep of Table~\ref{tab:deq} has a
generally dense adjoint source, so the reach proposition supplies no separation
there; it solves an $18$-dimensional system, making $K=32$ past the conservative
saturation bound. The whole table is read as a property of the Neumann rule.
The training probe of Section~\ref{sec:deq} is a $16$-node chain with scalar
blocks, and its $K$ denotes Neumann terms, hence $K-1$ products. At $K=16$ the
available powers can span the full grade-$16$ space, but the rule has spent
only $15$ products and is not past the generic solver/query ceiling. Only
$K=32$ is beyond that ceiling. The preregistered hard cells
$K\in\{1,2,4\}$ remain well below it. Its adjoint source is also
generally dense, so these cells do not inherit a routed-field reach claim. The
routed-field probe is $36$-dimensional with $K=7$ matched at the training size
and then held fixed, reaching $6$ of $10$ hops at the training size and $6$ of $94$ at the largest
test size, so it is inside the informative range by a wide margin and is the
one place where the conclusion binds the covered class rather than one rule.

For a fixed operator and source, the Krylov-span statement also gives a
sharper geometric floor. If $Q_P$ has orthonormal columns spanning
$\mathcal K_{P+1}(M,c)$ and $y=A^{-1}b$, then every covered output satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \|x-y\|_2^2 \geq \|(I-Q_PQ_P^{\mathsf T})y\|_2^2.
  \label{eq:krylov-projection-floor}
\end{equation}
Because this Krylov space is contained in the hop-supported coordinate space,
Equation~\eqref{eq:krylov-projection-floor} is at least as strong as the
support floor for that fixed $M$. It is not a uniform learned-model
certificate here: the routed-field model learns $M$, so the projection space
changes with its parameters. Projecting onto the label-generating operator or
one trained operator would not lower-bound every competitor. The numerical
class-wide certificate below therefore uses only support, which is uniform
over all learned edge weights with the stated graph pattern.

\subsection{From support to loss}
\label{app:floor}

Proposition~\ref{prop:reach} constrains support, while attainability is a
statement about loss. The step between them needs no new experiment. A covered
source-Krylov rule with $P$ products predicts zero at every vertex outside
$\hop_{P}(\supp b)$, so its squared error there is the exact field's own value.
Averaging over the test set $\mathcal{D}$ of $n$-node instances,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:floor}
\mathrm{MSE}(x)\;\geq\;F(P)\;:=\;\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|\,n}
\sum_{(A,b)\in\mathcal{D}}\;\sum_{v\,:\,d(v,\,\supp b)>P}
\bigl(\inv{A}b\bigr)_{v}^{2}.
\end{equation}
The bound holds for every covered rule at any parameter count and after any
amount of training, and $F(P)$ is computed from the labels alone: nothing is
fitted, and no arm has to be run to evaluate it. Density of $\inv{A}$ on its own
would not give a far-field error this large, since inverse entries can decay with
distance \citep{demko1984decay,benzi2007decay}; \eqref{eq:floor} instead measures
the omitted target mass directly on the instances used here.

Jacobi output has $P=6$ and a floor of $1.69\times10^{-2}$, which is $1460$
times the exact arm's test MSE of
$1.16\times10^{-5}$. The FLOP model nevertheless charges all seven products,
including the first product with zero. A generic covered competitor should
therefore receive $P=7$ useful products. Its more generous floor is
$6.69\times10^{-3}$, still $578$ times the exact test MSE, even before charging
GMRES orthogonalization or other method-specific overhead. Thus no rule in the
covered source-generated class attains the exact test error at the
seven-product budget, whatever its parameters and however long it trains.

At $K=1$ the trained arm reaches $2.984\times10^{-1}$ against a floor of

predict-the-mean is therefore a measurement and not a corollary of the
proposition, and the dose-response at $K\in\{16,32\}$ is carried by measurement
alone. Table~\ref{tab:reach-floor} marks the boundary of what the proposition
buys: it settles non-attainment against the exact arm for the whole covered
class, and it says nothing at the two largest budgets.

size. Table~\ref{tab:maze-compute-scaling} recomputes the same FLOP model at

\section{Confirmatory evidence chronology}
\label{app:integrity}

At repository commit \evidencecommit{}, the matched-fit protocol was frozen
on 29 June 2026, before the first aggregate read of HOLDOUT seeds 1000--1029.
The evidentiary records are \href{https://github.com/vanshsehrawat14/gabp-sparse-inv/blob/main/archive/docs/CONFIRMATORY_RESULTS.md}
{\nolinkurl{archive/docs/CONFIRMATORY_RESULTS.md}} and
\href{https://github.com/vanshsehrawat14/gabp-sparse-inv/tree/main/archive/results/CONFIRMATORY/matched_fit_taskA/}
{\nolinkurl{archive/results/CONFIRMATORY/matched_fit_taskA/}}; only these support the
paper's confirmatory interpretation.

On 30 June, a later branch froze a bootstrap protocol and reused the same
HOLDOUT seeds. Its assertion that the seeds were untouched is false in
wall-clock chronology, so that use is not an independent confirmation and
its verdict and effect-size claims are excluded. Both protocol files retain
dated provenance notes.

The first-read \texttt{summary.json} records a top-level
\texttt{run\_tag=CONFIRMATORY}, the HOLDOUT seed list, and a confirmatory
output path, but two nested payloads say \texttt{EXPLORATORY}. In
\href{https://github.com/vanshsehrawat14/gabp-sparse-inv/blob/main/archive/bench/matched_fit_taskA.py}
{\nolinkurl{archive/bench/matched_fit_taskA.py}}, those labels come from a module-level
constant; seed guarding and output routing use the confirmatory function
argument. This metadata defect does not change a metric and remains disclosed
in the frozen record.

The maze record is ambiguous in a different way. Its \texttt{floor\_sound} field
is \texttt{false}: at the in-distribution size, the $K=256$ floor arm's mean
evaluation-loss gap from exact is $2.70\times10^{-5}$, versus the
implementation's $3.07\times10^{-6}$ threshold. Results prose nevertheless
marked maze NC2 as passing because its mean absolute extrapolation gaps were
3.8--4.6 orders below the $K=7$ gaps, the narrower check implemented by
\texttt{confirmatory\_decision}. The preregistration required collapse in
``function/extrapolation distance'' and did not specify which of these
non-equivalent summaries controlled. This manuscript does not retroactively
choose the favorable one: maze NC2 is ambiguous, and no positive conclusion
depends on treating it as a pass.

The DEQ floor has a more serious structural problem, found in a 2026-07-25
re-audit of the frozen adjudication code. The preregistration defines
$F=\max(2M_{\mathrm{null}},2\times10^{-6})$, where
$M_{\mathrm{null}}$ is the near-exact cell's own matched function distance,
and applies $F$ to both that cell's C2 collapse test and the rival
inductive-bias branch. At high difficulty, $K=32$ is the only budget reaching
the $60\%$ matchability bar, and it reaches it in both cells ($22/30$ at
$\rho=0.98$ and $19/30$ at $\rho=0.99$), so those two cells are the only
adjudicable equal-fit arms; each also sets its own $M_{\mathrm{null}}$, so each
distance is compared with twice itself.
In
\href{https://github.com/vanshsehrawat14/gabp-sparse-inv/blob/main/archive/bench/matched_fit_taskA.py}
{\nolinkurl{archive/bench/matched_fit_taskA.py}}, the verdict is \textsc{survive} when
the matched distance's confidence interval (CI) lower bound exceeds $F$,
\textsc{collapse} when its mean is at most $F$, and \textsc{ambiguous}
otherwise. With $F=2\times$ that mean, \textsc{collapse} is forced and
\textsc{survive} is unreachable. C2's recorded pass is therefore
uninformative, and the inductive-bias branch could not have fired on these
data regardless of the measurements. Measured instead against the
preregistration's absolute optimization-noise scale $2\times10^{-6}$,
calibrated on development seeds where the largest silent-cell matched distance
was $1.46\times10^{-6}$, the two near-exact \emph{mean} distances
($9.11\times10^{-4}$ at $\rho=0.98$ and $1.84\times10^{-4}$ at $\rho=0.99$)
are $456\times$ and $92\times$ larger, and the $\rho=0.98$ CI lower bound
($3.84\times10^{-4}$) also exceeds it.

The choice of location statistic reverses that absolute-scale reading. Both
matched distributions are strongly right-skewed. Their medians are
$5.47\times10^{-8}$ and $3.26\times10^{-8}$, which are $37\times$ and
$61\times$ \emph{below} the same absolute scale; the mean therefore exceeds
the median by factors of $1.7\times10^{4}$ and $5.6\times10^{3}$. On the mean
the near-exact arms sit above the absolute floor, and on the median they
collapse beneath it. The $\rho=0.99$ interval spans the floor outright
($[7.75\times10^{-8},\,5.51\times10^{-4}]$, from $26\times$ below it to
$276\times$ above), so that cell does not decide the question in either
direction. With $n=22$ and $n=19$ matched seeds, these data cannot separate a
genuine equal-fit difference concentrated in a few seeds from a well-behaved
collapse with a heavy tail.

The preregistration specified the mean, so I report the frozen mean-based
verdict unchanged. The absolute-scale sensitivity cannot rescue what the
self-referential floor failed to settle. The evidence leaves no inductive-bias
comparison where the finite arms cannot fit, and leaves the question untested
where they can.
The DEQ archive retains per-cell aggregates rather than seed-level matched-fit
payloads. I therefore did not reconstruct its counts and intervals from
individual records or rerun the retired HOLDOUT population. Only
\texttt{armA\_matched\_fdist} carries a median, so this robustness check cannot
extend to the as-trained separations quoted elsewhere.

The same two $K=32$ cells are also past the useful budget range. The training
system is a $16$-node chain with scalar blocks, while this arm has 32 Neumann terms and
spends 31 products, which exceeds the dimension of the system being solved.
By Remark~\ref{rem:saturation}, a residual-optimal method has already reached
the exact-termination ceiling at that budget, and the cell cannot distinguish
a budget effect from a property of the Neumann polynomial even had the floor
been sound. The self-referential floor and saturated budget are separate
problems, and either is enough to void the cell. The
preregistered hard cells $K\in\{1,2,4\}$ are at most a quarter of the dimension
and reach at most $3$ of the chain's $15$ hops, so the conservative reading does
not depend on either defect. Any successor experiment comparing solve rules at
a matched product budget must be run at $n\gg P_{\max}$; at $n\le P_{\max}$ the
strongest competitor degenerates into the exact arm and the comparison is
vacuous by construction.

\paragraph{Fresh GMRES record.}
The post-hoc adversarial study was frozen before development seeds 4000--4019
